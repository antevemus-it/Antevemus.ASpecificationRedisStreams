# Tests

The suite runs with PHPUnit in three groups, `Unit`, `Contract` and `Integration`, in the CI of every push on PHP 8.2, 8.3 and 8.4, with a `redis:7.4-alpine` service:

```bash
ASPEC_REDIS_HOST=127.0.0.1 ASPEC_REDIS_PORT=6379 vendor/bin/phpunit
```

The tests that need Redis are opt-in: without `ASPEC_REDIS_HOST`, or when the server does not answer, they are skipped with the reason (with `ASPEC_REDIS_REQUIRED=1`, as in the CI, they fail instead). They use the database `ASPEC_REDIS_DATABASE` (default 15), each under a prefix of keys of its own, deleted at the end. `phpunit.xml.dist` shows how to start a server for them in a container.

## What is tested

- **The contract battery of the core.** Since 1.0.1 the contract of the messaging hook is not a battery of this package: `RedisStreamsContractTest` extends `MessagingContractTestCase`, the public battery that `antevemus/aspecification` 2.15 ships for every broker satellite, and runs it against this package on a real Redis 7.4, through `RedisStreamsBench` (an `IMessagingBench`: a prefix of keys of its own per bench, deleted at the end; another member of the group that recovers by `XAUTOCLAIM` what a dead one held, after an idle time of 40 ms; the same member started again; a client of a port where nothing listens for the lost connection; an entry written by another producer; the dead letter stream of the group read back). The core runs the same battery against its in-memory reference. Every scenario goes the whole way of the core (`MessagePublisher` into an in-memory outbox, `OutboxRelay` through the transport, `MessageConsumer` with an in-memory inbox, the inbox row and the effects of the handlers committed together) and compares the transcript, the effects, the reasons and the dead letters with literals, the order within each ordering key and the multiset of the rest. The 22 scenarios: the round trip, to the microsecond; every consumer getting every message; the order of a key across names, across batches, in batches of mixed keys and in a batch of many keys; a delivery given back coming again before the later ones of its key (in one run, with deliveries left behind, one delivery at a time); the dead letter after `maxDeliveries`, with its reason and count; a body that is not a message; a name without handler; a consumer of one tenant; the largest payload, 65 535 bytes, byte for byte; duplicates absorbed by the inbox; a relay that dies between the confirmation and the mark; the deliveries of a dead consumer going to its successor; a process started again; a lost connection failing the whole batch; the secrecy of every reason, report and exception; each bench a world of its own; and a property sequence of random publications, relays that die or lose the connection, consumers that die before an acknowledgement (started again or replaced by another member), handlers that fail once, twice or always, and copies stored by the broker, with 6 fixed seeds × 150 steps, after which no confirmed message is lost, no effect ran twice, the order of every key held and the dead letters of the broker are those of the reports. How the battery works and how to write a bench for another broker: [Writing a satellite](https://github.com/antevemus-it/Antevemus.ASpecification/blob/main/docs/en/messaging/writing-a-satellite.md), in the manual of the core.
- **Redis itself** (`RedisStreamsTransportTest`, `RedisStreamsSourceTest`): the fields of an entry and of a dead letter, the global order across batches and streams (a promise of this package, beyond the order within a key that the battery asserts), the approximate cap, a refused message failing alone with the error code (`WRONGTYPE`, `NOPERM` through a user of the ACL that may not run the command), a lost connection; the group created once and kept, from the end or from the start; two members sharing a group and another group getting everything; the merge of the streams by `seq` and not by the entry ids; each stream read just far enough (the pending entries counted after each receive); the entries read and not handed out keeping their count; the blocking wait and its cap; a delivery given back claimed again at once, a hundred times in a row; at most `$max` claimed per stream, listed past what is in flight and what is read and not handed out in their stream; the dead letters of a group, in order, limited and paged; the idle recovery, its walk through the pending entries of the group and its absence when disabled; an entry deleted while pending (by this member and by a dead one); an entry without body; a connection killed by the server while answering; and a refusal of Redis at each step of a receive, of an acknowledgement, of a dead letter and of the reading of the dead letters, each with its exact message.
- **Pure rules**: the topology and every boundary of its values, the order of the candidates and the fields of an entry (`StreamEntries`), the one round trip of a pipeline and the texts of a failure (`RedisCommands`).
- **The documentation:** every PHP block of `docs/en` and `docs/pt-BR`, and the Quick Start of each README, runs with the answers written in its comments; the blocks that talk to Redis run against the test server, the address written in them replaced by its own. Both languages carry the same code and the same files.

## Mutation testing

Infection runs over the whole `src/` in a scheduled workflow, against a Redis service, with minimum scores one point below the last measure. Measured for 1.0.1 with one thread: 378 mutants, 370 killed, 6 time-outs, 2 escaped, both equivalent (the count of an `XPENDING` over the range of one entry id; the minimum idle time of the `XCLAIM` of a delivery given back, 0, turned into -1, which Redis reads as 0); MSI 99.47%, mutation code coverage 100%. The workflow runs with one thread too, because with parallel threads the score reads higher (99.74% for 1.0.1): the blocks of the documentation that talk to Redis share the keys of the default prefix and a race between two processes can kill a mutant; the mutant it may leave is the minimum idle time of that `XCLAIM` turned into 1 ms, which differs only when the rejection and the next receive fall in the same millisecond of the server, a case one test tries a hundred times but cannot force under load. For 1.0.0: 378 mutants, 374 killed, 1 time-out, 3 escaped; MSI 99.21%.

The suite has 142 tests (on PHP 8.4 and 8.2, with Redis 7.4: 4 548 assertions); without a Redis server, 94 of them are skipped.

# Tests

The suite runs with PHPUnit in four groups, `Unit`, `Contract`, `Integration` and `Property`, in the CI of every push on PHP 8.2, 8.3 and 8.4, with a `redis:7.4-alpine` service:

```bash
ASPEC_REDIS_HOST=127.0.0.1 ASPEC_REDIS_PORT=6379 vendor/bin/phpunit
```

The tests that need Redis are opt-in: without `ASPEC_REDIS_HOST`, or when the server does not answer, they are skipped with the reason (with `ASPEC_REDIS_REQUIRED=1`, as in the CI, they fail instead). They use the database `ASPEC_REDIS_DATABASE` (default 15), each under a prefix of keys of its own, deleted at the end. `phpunit.xml.dist` shows how to start a server for them in a container.

## What is tested

- **One contract battery, two brokers.** The same scenarios, written once, run against the in-memory broker of the core (`InMemoryMessageTransport` and `InMemoryMessageSource`, the reference) and against this package on a real Redis 7.4, and must give the same transcript (`<message> <outcome> <delivery count>` per delivery, the effects of the handlers, the dead letters). Every scenario goes the whole way of the core: `MessagePublisher` into the in-memory outbox, `OutboxRelay` through the transport, `MessageConsumer` with the in-memory inbox, the inbox row and the effects of the handlers committed together. The scenarios: publish, relay and consume, the messages arriving as published to the microsecond; the order of two keys across the streams of two names; a delivery given back coming again before the later ones of its key (several at a time and one at a time); the dead letter after `maxDeliveries`, with its reason and count; an invalid body; a name without handler; a consumer restricted to a tenant; a payload of exactly 65 535 bytes; a batch of mixed keys and messages without key; duplicates absorbed by the inbox; a crash between the confirmation of the broker and the mark of the outbox (sent again, the effect once); a member that died with deliveries in flight, recovered by another one; a process started again with the same member; a lost connection failing the whole batch and marking nothing; and the secrecy of every reason, report and exception, with a payload that carries a marker.
- **Redis itself** (`RedisStreamsTransportTest`, `RedisStreamsSourceTest`): the fields of an entry and of a dead letter, the global order across batches and streams, the approximate cap, a refused message failing alone with the error code (`WRONGTYPE`, `NOPERM` through a user of the ACL that may not run the command), a lost connection; the group created once and kept, from the end or from the start; two members sharing a group and another group getting everything; the merge of the streams by `seq` and not by the entry ids; each stream read just far enough (the pending entries counted after each receive); the entries read and not handed out keeping their count; the blocking wait and its cap; a delivery given back claimed again at once, a hundred times in a row; at most `$max` claimed per stream; the dead letters of a group, in order, limited and paged; the idle recovery, its walk through the pending entries of the group and its absence when disabled; an entry deleted while pending (by this member and by a dead one); an entry without body; a connection killed by the server while answering; and a refusal of Redis at each step of a receive, of an acknowledgement, of a dead letter and of the reading of the dead letters, each with its exact message.
- **A property over random interleavings** (`MessagingPropertyTest`): 3 fixed seeds × 150 steps, against Redis and against the reference of the core. Each step publishes 1 to 3 messages (two names, three keys and no key; one in twenty a poison message that always fails, three in twenty failing once), relays a random number of them in random batches (one relay in six dies between the confirmation and the mark), consumes a random number (one run in ten dies before an acknowledgement, after the commit, and the process starts again with the same member), or makes the broker store a confirmed message again. After a drain: every published message was handled or dead-lettered (none lost), none was handled twice, the messages of each key were handled in the order published (a key with a dead letter left out), every run reported the dead letters it made, and the dead letters of the broker are exactly those. Each seed reaches crashes of the relay, deaths of the consumer, copies of the broker, held-back deliveries, duplicates absorbed and dead letters.
- **Pure rules**: the topology and every boundary of its values, the order of the candidates and the fields of an entry (`StreamEntries`), the one round trip of a pipeline and the texts of a failure (`RedisCommands`).
- **The documentation:** every PHP block of `docs/en` and `docs/pt-BR`, and the Quick Start of each README, runs with the answers written in its comments; the blocks that talk to Redis run against the test server, the address written in them replaced by its own. Both languages carry the same code and the same files.

## Mutation testing

Infection runs over the whole `src/` in a scheduled workflow, against a Redis service, with minimum scores one point below the last measure. Measured for 1.0.0: 378 mutants, 374 killed, 1 time-out, 3 escaped, all equivalent (the count of an `XPENDING` over the range of one entry id; the minimum idle time of the `XCLAIM` of a delivery given back, 0, turned into -1, which Redis reads as 0, or into 1 ms, which differs only when the rejection and the next receive fall in the same millisecond of the server, a case one test tries a hundred times but cannot force under the load of a mutation run); MSI 99.21%, mutation code coverage 100%.

The suite has 152 tests (on PHP 8.4 and 8.2, with Redis 7.4: 3 947 assertions); without a Redis server, 85 of them are skipped.
